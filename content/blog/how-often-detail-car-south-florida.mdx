Most car care advice on the internet is written for places with four seasons. It assumes your paint's worst enemy is road salt in January and pollen in April. South Florida operates on completely different rules, and following a national detailing schedule here is how cars end up with oxidized clear coat at four years old.

We detail vehicles across Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach every week. What follows is the schedule we actually recommend to customers, and the reasoning behind it.

## The three things destroying your paint down here

### Salt air reaches much further inland than people think

Airborne salt doesn't stop at the beach. Onshore winds carry salt aerosol several miles inland, which means a car parked in Coral Gables or Plantation is getting a light salt deposit most days — just less of it than one parked in [Miami Beach](/miami-beach/mobile-car-detailing).

Salt itself doesn't eat paint. What it does is hold moisture against the surface and accelerate corrosion anywhere the clear coat is already compromised — rock chips, door edges, the lip under your trunk lid. Those small failures turn into rust blooms far faster here than they would in Atlanta.

### UV exposure is year-round and relentless

South Florida sits at a UV index of 10 or 11 for much of the year. There is no winter reprieve. UV breaks down the clear coat's polymer structure continuously, and once oxidation starts, it isn't reversible by washing — the only fix is machine polishing to remove the dead layer, which you can only do so many times before there isn't enough clear coat left.

This is the single strongest argument for [ceramic coating](/services/ceramic-coating) in this market. It's not about the shine. It's about putting a sacrificial UV barrier between the sun and a finish you can't replace.

### Afternoon rain is not a free car wash

Rainwater picks up airborne contaminants on the way down and leaves them on your paint when it evaporates. In summer, that cycle runs almost daily: wet, bake dry in direct sun, repeat. Those mineral deposits bond hard, and by the time they're visible as water spots they've often already begun etching.

## The schedule that actually works

How often you need service depends on two variables that matter far more than what you drive: **where the car sleeps**, and **how far it is from the water**.

| Situation | Maintenance detail | Paint protection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outdoors, within ~3 miles of coast | Every 4 weeks | Ceramic coating, reapply per spec |
| Outdoors, inland | Every 6–8 weeks | Ceramic or sealant twice yearly |
| Garage-kept, coastal | Every 6–8 weeks | Ceramic coating recommended |
| Garage-kept, inland | Every 8–12 weeks | Sealant twice yearly |

A "maintenance detail" here means a proper hand wash, decontamination as needed, and interior service — not a tunnel wash. Automatic washes with spinning brushes are the leading cause of the swirl marks we spend hours correcting.

### Add a seasonal layer

Two moments in the South Florida calendar deserve their own attention:

**Late May through June — love bug season.** Love bug remains are acidic. On a hot panel in direct sun, they can etch clear coat in 24 to 48 hours. During these weeks, don't wait for your scheduled appointment; rinse the front end and windshield as soon as you can. If etching has already started, [paint correction](/services/paint-correction) is the fix, and the sooner the better.

**October through November — post-hurricane season.** Storm exposure means wind-driven salt spray, debris impacts, and standing water. A decontamination service after the season closes catches damage while it's still cosmetic.

## What we'd skip

A few things get recommended constantly and earn their cost less often than people assume:

- **Waxing every month.** Traditional carnauba wax lasts roughly six weeks in this climate — less on a car parked in full sun. You're paying repeatedly for protection that's gone before the next application. A sealant or coating is better value.
- **Engine bay cleaning on a schedule.** Worth doing occasionally, especially before a sale, but it isn't preventive maintenance for most drivers.
- **Interior protectant on every surface.** Useful on dash and door tops, which take direct UV. Largely cosmetic elsewhere.

## The short version

If your car lives outside near the water, plan on a maintenance detail every four weeks and put a real protective coating on the paint. If it lives in a garage inland, you can relax to every couple of months. Everything else — the seasonal timing, the products, the extras — is a refinement on those two decisions.

We bring everything with us, including water and power, so none of this requires dropping the car off somewhere. Most of our [mobile detailing](/services/mobile-car-detailing) appointments happen in a driveway, an office lot, or a condo garage while the owner is doing something else.
